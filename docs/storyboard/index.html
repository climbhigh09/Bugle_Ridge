Bugle
Ridge

An offline spot-and-stalk elk game for the long sits. Glass the hillside, plan the stalk, call or creep, make the shot, then hear from an old outfitter what went wrong.

One hunt day

Eight screens, about five minutes

Every in-game day follows this order, but most days end before screen 6, just like real hunting. The game saves at every step, so you can put the phone down the second a real elk steps out.

The campaign

Four seasons, one trail

Each state is a chapter with its own terrain, how the elk behave, and one new skill. You carry your gear and camp cash forward. If you eat your tag, you can hunt the season again with what you learned.

Weapons progression

Gear that fixes a real problem

The shop only suggests an upgrade after a debrief shows you needed it. Numbers come from standard formulas and typical factory loads. The game tunes around them but keeps them honest.

The mentor loop

Hank's debrief drives the upgrades

Each debrief pins the failure on one cause and gives two fixes: a skill fix that's always free, and a gear fix only when gear was really the limit. You buy upgrades with camp cash, not by grinding kills.

"She never saw you. She smelled you. The sun hit that slope at 9:40 and your wind went uphill."

Built for the hill

Light on battery, quiet in the field

Rendering
The game draws on a 180×320 pixel canvas that's scaled up without blurring, which makes each frame very cheap to draw.
Frame rate
Menus and maps redraw only when you touch them. Stalking and shooting screens are capped at 30 fps.
Palette
Mostly near-black, so most OLED pixels stay off.
Ridge mode
Turns the app's own brightness down to a dim floor so your face doesn't glow on the hill.
Sound
Silent by default. There's one short buzz when an elk shows up in your binos.
Offline
No network, no ads, no tracking. The only permission it asks for is vibration.
Saving
Saves on every decision and whenever you leave the app, so you can reopen mid-stalk.
Target
Aim for 4% battery or less per 30 minutes in Ridge mode. I'll measure it on your phone.

Build order

  1. Colorado archery slice. Glass, plan, stalk, call, shoot, and debrief across five days, with two upgrades. Installed as an APK so you can play it on a real sit.
  2. Colorado rifle season, shop, and camp jobs. Cartridges, holdover, and suppressor mechanics.
  3. Idaho and Montana. Canyon range estimation, pressure from other hunters, long pack-outs.
  4. Alaska moose finale. Judging whether a bull is legal, calling moose, the float trip, and bears.

Still open

  • Should season dates be loosely realistic, or match the current regulations for each state?
  • Should a cow tag count as a win, or should the campaign keep score in bulls?
